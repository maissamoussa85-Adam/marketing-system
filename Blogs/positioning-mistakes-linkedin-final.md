# Why your homepage is lying to your buyers (and your sales team wrote it)

In 13 years running demand gen and ABM for B2B SaaS companies, I've seen one mistake cost teams more pipeline than any budget cut, channel failure, or bad quarter.

It's not the ad spend. It's not the content volume. It's not even the product.

It's letting your sales team write your positioning for you.

---

## The mistake hiding in plain sight

Here's how it happens.

Sales gets pushback on a pricing objection, so they ask marketing to address it on the site. Marketing adds a paragraph. Then another objection comes in. Then another. This happens 40 times over 18 months.

Now your homepage is a collection of every deal you've ever lost, dressed up as a value proposition. It reads like it was written by a committee. Because it was.

Sales input is useful signal, not output.

Let sales tell you where messaging breaks down. But the fix should come from stepping back to three fundamental questions: who is this for, what do they want to change, and why are you the right choice? Answer those first. Then let the objections shape the tone, not the structure.

---

## The second mistake: positioning is not a one-time project

I've watched this pattern repeat across every company I've worked with.

Leadership hires a consultant, runs a positioning workshop, rewrites the website, and calls it done. Two years later, AI has commoditised three of their key features, a new competitor entered with a simpler message, and the positioning hasn't moved. The market did. The messaging didn't.

Here's the fix I use with teams: block two hours every quarter with whoever owns messaging and work through four questions:

1. Which features did we ship or deprecate? Does our messaging reflect what we actually do today?
2. Which competitors have repositioned? Are we still meaningfully distinct?
3. Which customer segments are closing fastest, and does our copy speak to them?
4. Where is sales improvising on calls? That's where messaging is breaking down.

If more than two of those point to a gap, your positioning needs to move. It doesn't have to be a full workshop. A shared doc and two hours is enough to catch drift before it costs you deals.

---

## The third mistake: confusing awareness with demand

Take your last five LinkedIn posts. How many of them made someone think differently about their problem? How many just reminded them you exist?

Here's the difference:

Awareness post: "We just launched a new feature. Here's how it works."

Demand post: "Most B2B SaaS companies think their pipeline problem is a volume problem. It's not. It's a positioning problem."

Same company. Same week. Very different effect. The first post reaches people. The second changes minds. If your last five posts are all product updates and announcements, you're building brand awareness with no one who's ready to buy yet.

---

## A quick self-serve move you can do this week

Email your three best recent customers and ask them one question: "What were you most worried about before you decided to work with us?"

Their answers will tell you more about your real positioning than any workshop, consultant, or rebrand ever could.
