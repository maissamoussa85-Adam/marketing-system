## Individual feedback

### Sarah Chen - Skeptical Senior Buyer (CMO, Series B, $8M ARR)

**First impression:** This reads like someone who's actually been in the room when these conversations happen -- it's not recycled HubSpot content, and it doesn't insult my intelligence. But it stops just short of being genuinely useful.

**What resonates:** Mistake 5 hit hardest, and I'm a little annoyed it did, because it's exactly the wound I have. I've paid for "senior strategy" and gotten 26-year-olds writing my LinkedIn copy based on a one-hour onboarding call. The framing of awareness vs. demand (Mistake 2) is also something I wish I'd had language for three years ago when I was fighting internally about why 50,000 impressions on a campaign didn't move pipeline. And the campaign-vs-program distinction is real -- I've watched our own team reset to zero every quarter because that's how the budget cycle works, not because it's the right model.

**What doesn't land:** The checklist at the end is filler -- if I've read this far, I don't need five checkboxes to tell me whether I recognize myself in it. More importantly, Mistake 3 on messaging drift is the weakest section. It correctly names the problem but gives me nothing I can actually do. "Build a regular process for stress-testing your messaging" is not advice. What does that process look like? Who's in the room? What triggers a review? Also, the CTA -- "it's worth a conversation" -- is exactly the soft, hedged close that junior people write when they don't want to seem pushy. If this post is arguing that I need partners who think like senior buyers, the CTA should model that confidence.

**One question:** What does a demand generation program that isn't making these mistakes actually look like in practice -- specifically for a company at my stage ($5M-$15M ARR, Series B, limited headcount)? Give me one concrete example of what "running a program instead of campaigns" looked like for a real company, with real numbers, and I'll take the next meeting myself.

**Score:** 3.5/5

**One question:** If I wanted to shift from campaign-based to program-based demand gen starting next quarter, what does the first 90 days actually look like -- what do I stop, what do I start, and how do I keep leadership from pulling the plug when they don't see immediate pipeline movement?

## Top 3 improvements

Based on all feedback, the three highest-priority improvements are:

1. **Make the "what to do instead" sections specific and actionable** -- All three personas flagged this independently. Every mistake is diagnosed well but the fix is too vague to be useful. Marcus put it best: "it stops right before the useful part every single time." Each mistake needs at least one concrete, specific action -- not a principle.

2. **Add a real-world example with numbers** -- Sarah and Pieter both asked for this directly. The campaign-vs-program point (Mistake 1) is the strongest in the post and would carry three times the weight with one concrete client example: what they were doing, what shifted, and what happened to pipeline. Even directional numbers would work. This is the difference between "interesting read" and "I'm booking a call."

3. **Rewrite the CTA with confidence** -- All three found "it's worth a conversation" too hedged. Sarah called it out explicitly: the post argues for senior-level confidence but closes like a junior afraid to ask for the meeting. The CTA should be direct about who this is for and what the next step actually is.
