## Individual feedback

### Sandra -- Skeptical Senior Buyer (CMO, 18 years experience)

**First impression:** This reads like it was written by someone who's actually been in a room where a positioning project stalled -- it's not generic advice dressed up in B2B language, which is rare. My guard went up at the headline and then slowly came down as I kept reading.

**What resonates:** Mistake 3 hit me directly. We ran a positioning workshop 18 months ago, rewrote the website, and called it done. The framing of "AI has commoditized three of your key features" is exactly what happened to us -- we built differentiation into capabilities that are now table stakes, and our messaging never moved. The quarterly check cadence is practical enough that I could actually propose it internally without it sounding like another big initiative. Mistake 5 also landed hard. Our homepage is a graveyard of objections our sales team escalated over two years. I've never heard it described that cleanly before, but that's exactly what happened.

**What doesn't land:** The CTA at the end is vague. "Start with a conversation" tells me nothing. I'm a CMO who's been burned twice by agencies -- that line reads like every other agency that promised a GTM engine and delivered a deck. If Unmuted is positioning themselves as the antidote to fuzzy marketing, the way they sell themselves at the bottom of this post is fuzzy marketing. Also, Mistake 4 is the weakest section. The advice -- "does this change how someone thinks about their problem?" -- sounds good but doesn't actually help me audit my current content plan.

**One question:** We've done the workshop. We have the positioning doc. The problem isn't that we don't know what good positioning looks like -- it's that it never makes it into the actual content, the sales deck, or the SDR sequences. How do you get positioning to stick operationally, not just on paper?

**Score:** 4/5 -- I'd send it to my VP of Demand Gen with a note that says "read Mistake 3 and Mistake 5."

### Marcus -- Hands-On Implementer (Head of Demand Generation)

**First impression:** Better than most positioning content I wade through, but it front-loads diagnosis and leaves me doing most of the work on the fix. The core framing is solid, but "treat positioning as a living document" is advice I've heard a hundred times.

**What resonates:** Mistake 5 hit close to home. We absolutely have a homepage that accreted objection responses over 18 months of sales asking for "just one more thing." The framing of sales input as signal not output is genuinely useful language I can take into the next conversation with our AE team without sounding like I'm dismissing them. Mistake 2 also lands hard -- we wrote our first real messaging when we had 12 customers in one industry, and we're now selling to three different buyer personas and the copy still sounds like it was written for that first cohort.

**What doesn't land:** The quarterly positioning audit advice is where it falls apart for me. You say "run a quarterly check" but there's no structure for how to actually do that. What questions do I ask? Who's in the room? What data do I bring? "Revisit it every quarter" is advice I already know and have failed to operationalize. Mistake 4 on awareness vs. demand also needed more. The guidance on ratios is hand-wavy. "In the right ratio for your stage" is not actionable.

**One question:** If I wanted to run a positioning audit next month -- just me, maybe one sales leader, no outside consultant -- what does that actually look like as a two-hour working session? What inputs do I need, what do I walk out with, and how do I know if my positioning moved in the right direction?

**Score:** 3/5 -- I'd file it as a good framing piece but I wouldn't forward it to my team because they'd finish it and ask "so what do we do now?"

## Top 3 improvements

1. **Rewrite the CTA with specific proof** -- All four personas flagged "Start with a conversation" as vague or trust-destroying. The CTA should reflect Unmuted's own positioning principles: name the before, name the after, and give a concrete next step. Add a client result or a specific outcome so it earns the ask.

2. **Make the quarterly audit operational** -- Sandra, Marcus, and Lena all know they should revisit positioning regularly but can't do it. The advice to "treat positioning as a living document" is not actionable for someone who has to run this process themselves. Add a short list of specific audit questions, or a two-hour session structure, so the advice is something readers can actually execute without hiring anyone.

3. **Give individual contributors a self-serve path** -- Lena's feedback surfaces a gap the senior personas didn't name: the blog assumes someone with budget and authority to act. Readers who are executors (marketing managers, demand gen specialists) finish the post and have nowhere to go. One concrete DIY step before the CTA -- even a simple framework or a set of interview questions to run with customers -- would serve this segment and make the post feel genuinely generous rather than a funnel with good camouflage.
